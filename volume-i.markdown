Before we go deep on any one of its dimensions, it makes the case for why depth is needed at all. **Volume I** is that case: it names the failure mode, looks closely at three of those dimensions — decisions, reasoning, and knowledge — and closes by naming what connects them.

This page is a hand-curated compilation of the articles that belong to Volume I, with a short note on how each one fits the arc. Publishing an article doesn't automatically add it here — that's a deliberate editorial step, separate from tagging.

### Articles in this volume

1. **[The Next Industrial Catastrophe Will Involve an Overconfident AI]({{ "/articles/2026/07/10/the-next-industrial-catastrophe/" | relative_url }})** 
   Names the failure: a confident wrong answer costs more than a refusal, and almost no AI system built today knows the difference.

2. **[The Ungoverned Middle]({{ "/articles/2026/07/12/the-ungoverned-middle/" | relative_url }})** 
   One seam of the problem, up close: the decision layer between prediction and execution, the Governed Decision Layer, and the "who signs?" test.

3. **[Correlation Runs the Dashboard. Causation Runs the Plant.]({{ "/articles/2026/07/14/correlation-runs-the-dashboard/" | relative_url }})** 
   A different seam of the same problem: why grounded reasoning, not just accurate prediction, is what a plant actually needs.

4. **[Engineering Trustworthy Industrial Decisions in the Age of AI]({{ "/articles/2026/07/25/engineering-trustworthy-industrial-decisions/" | relative_url }})** 
   The reveal: three dimensions — knowledge, reasoning, decisions — are what make a decision itself trustworthy. What it takes to operationalize and scale one is a separate arc, for later.
